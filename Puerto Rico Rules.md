# Puerto Rico — Rulebook

## Goal

Players assume different roles across multiple rounds to:

- Place plantations
- Build buildings
- Produce goods
- Sell or ship those goods

The objective is to achieve:

- The greatest prosperity
- The highest respect

This is measured by **Victory Points (VP)**.

**Winner:** The player with the most victory points at the end of the game.

---

## Game overview

The game is played over multiple rounds (approximately 15).

**Each round:**

- Players take turns selecting role cards
- For each role, the **chooser takes the action first**, then each other player in **clockwise** order (exception: Prospector has no action)

**Roles determine:**

- Available actions
- Special privileges

**Sidebar-style notes:** The official rulebook uses sidebars for summaries and **special circumstances**; this document includes those details inline.

## Setup (preparation)

- Place the game board centrally.
- Place all buildings on designated spaces.
- Sort and place doubloons on the bank.
- Return any unused components to the box.

### Supply setup

- **Plantation tiles:** Shuffle into 5 face-down stacks; reveal (players + 1) tiles face-up
- **Goods:** Sorted into 5 piles
- **Trading house:** Place nearby

  - 3 players: 55

### Cargo ships

- 3 players: 4, 5, 6 capacity
- 4 players: 5, 6, 7 capacity
- 5 players: 6, 7, 8 capacity

### Victory points

- 3 players: 75 VP
- 4 players: 100 VP
- 5 players: 122 VP

- 1 player board
- **Starting doubloons:**
  - 3 players: 2
  - 4 players: 3

## Round structure

Each round proceeds as follows:

1. Governor selects a role
2. All players perform the role’s action, clockwise
3. Next player selects a remaining role
4. Repeat until all roles are chosen

### End of round

- Place 1 doubloon on each unused role card (**doubloons stack** on a card across rounds, regardless of how many are already there — e.g. a Prospector with 2 on the card yields 3 total when taken)
- Return all used role cards
- Pass Governor card clockwise

---

## General role rules

- All players perform the action (exception: Prospector)
- Only the chooser receives the privilege
- The player who **chooses** a role may choose **not** to use that role’s **privilege** (and may also skip the **action** on their own turn, except Captain — see Captain phase). Other players still take the role’s action on their turns when applicable.
- The **action** is optional for each player on their turn (exception: Captain — must load when able)
- Players must take a role when it is their turn to choose
- Role cards cannot be reused in the same round
- If a role has doubloons on it: the chooser collects them **in addition** to privilege and action
- A role card stays with the player who took it until the end of the round

---

## Role phases

### Settler phase

#### Action

**Chooser (settler):** May take **either** the privilege (quarry) **or** one face-up plantation and place it on any empty island space.

**Each other player (clockwise):** May take **one face-up plantation** only (not a quarry) — **exception:** **Construction Hut** lets that player take a quarry instead of a face-up plantation, same as the rulebook special ability.

#### Privilege

The settler may take a **quarry** tile instead of a face-up plantation (if quarries remain).

#### End of phase

- Untaken face-up plantations go to the discard pile
- Reveal **(players + 1)** new face-up plantations from the stacks

#### Notes

- Remember **Hacienda**, **Construction Hut**, and **Hospice** in this phase
- Tile placement position is irrelevant
- Tiles cannot be removed
- If not enough tiles in the face-down stacks: draw what you can, then shuffle discards face-down into a new stack and refill; if still insufficient, later rounds may have fewer face-up tiles
- If a player has filled all **12 island spaces**, they may not place plantations or quarries in future settler phases
- If **no quarry tiles** remain: the settler **cannot** use the quarry privilege, and **Construction Hut** owners **cannot** use that building’s quarry ability

---

### Mayor phase

Tiles (plantation, quarry, building) have **1–3 circles**. If at least one colonist is on a tile, it is **occupied**. **Only occupied tiles function**; unoccupied tiles never use their abilities.

#### Action

Players:

- Take colonists from the colonist ship, **one at a time**, starting with the mayor
- Continue clockwise until the ship is empty

#### Privilege

The mayor:

- Takes 1 colonist from **supply** (not the ship) **before** the ship is distributed

#### Colonist placement

Place on any empty circle:

- Plantations
- Quarries
- Buildings

A player may place **new** colonists together with **all** colonists from earlier rounds on any empty circles — colonists may be **moved** from earlier placements or from **San Juan**. Unplaced colonists go to **San Juan** and stay there until a later mayor phase.

#### End of phase

- Count **empty circles on buildings only** (not plantations/quarries)
- Add that many colonists from supply to the ship
- **Minimum:** number of players (if the count would be lower, raise it to player count)

#### Notes

- **No San Juan dumping if circles exist:** You may not put colonists in San Juan if you have empty circles on your board; **all empty circles must be filled if possible**
- Colonists cannot be placed outside the mayor phase
- **Placement order:** Usually everyone places at once; if decisions depend on others, order is **mayor first**, then clockwise from the mayor
- If supply runs out: mayor gets no privilege and the ship is **not** refilled
- If mayor forgets to refill the ship: add at least **minimum (player count)** colonists to the ship when remembered

---

### Builder phase

#### Action

Each player:

- May build 1 building
- Pays cost to bank

#### Privilege

Builder:

- Pays 1 doubloon less

#### Rules

- Large buildings require 2 adjacent spaces
- Maximum: 12 city spaces
- One building per round

#### Quarry effect

Each occupied quarry reduces cost by 1. Max reduction depends on building column:


| Column | Max reduction |
| ------ | ------------- |
| 1      | 1             |
| 2      | 2             |
| 3      | 3             |
| 4      | 4             |


- Total cost cannot go below 0
- Builder privilege stacks with quarry reduction

**Example (3 occupied quarries):** Construction Hut 1 doubloon; Office 3; Harbor 5; City Hall 7 (column limits still apply).

#### Notes

- Remember **University** (builder phase effect)
- If builder does not build → **no** 1-doubloon privilege discount
- No empty city space → cannot build

---

### Craftsman phase

#### Action

Each player produces goods based on:

- Occupied plantations
- Occupied production buildings

#### Privilege

Craftsman:

- Takes 1 additional good

#### Notes

- Remember **Factory**
- If a **kind** of good is exhausted in the supply, the player gets **none** of that kind
- If the craftsman produces **no** goods, **no** extra good (privilege)
- **Designer note:** Craftsman is often the **riskiest** role — you may help opponents more than yourself

---

### Trader phase

#### Action

Each player:

- May sell 1 good

#### Privilege

Trader:

- Earns +1 doubloon

#### Trading rules

- Trading house capacity: 4 goods
- Only different goods allowed (exception: Office)
- Phase ends when all players acted, or house is full

#### End of phase

- If full: empty trading house
- Otherwise: goods **remain** — the next trader phase is harder (fewer spaces and kinds already present)

#### Notes

- Remember **Small Market**, **Large Market**, and **Office**
- Corn may be sold for 0 doubloons
- If trader does not sell → no +1 doubloon privilege

---

### Captain phase

#### Action

The phase continues clockwise as long as **at least one player** can still load. On **your** turn to load, you **must** load **if you can**. You may load **only one kind** of good per turn.

#### Privilege

Captain earns **+1 VP once**, on the **first** kind of good they load in the phase — not per kind. If the captain loads **no** goods, **no** privilege VP.

#### Shipping rules

- Each ship holds **one kind** of good only
- **No duplicate kinds across ships** (two ships cannot hold the same good type)
- Cannot load onto a **full** ship
- On a turn, you must load **as many as you can** of the chosen kind — you cannot hold back if there is space on a ship already carrying that kind
- **Choosing among ships (same good):** If your good could go on more than one non-full ship, you must choose a ship where you load the **maximum** possible (ideally leaving none behind). *Example:* 6 sugar and ships of 5 and 7 spaces (both empty for sugar) — you must use the **7**-space ship to load all 6, not the 5.
- **Choosing among goods (different kinds):** If you could load **different kinds**, you may **choose freely** which kind to load — you need not pick the kind that ships the most barrels
- **Mandatory load vs Wharf:** On your turn you **must** load onto a **cargo ship** whenever you legally can — **unless** you use your **Wharf** for that turn, in which case you send one kind to supply instead (see Wharf).
- **Harbor** and **Wharf** (see Buildings): remember their captain-phase effects

#### Victory points

- **1 VP per barrel** loaded (trader prices do not apply)
- VP chips are kept **secret** (face-down on windrose); players should exchange five **1** chips for one **5** when practical

#### Storage rules

When **no one** can load further, each player stores goods still on their windrose:

- **Default:** keep **1** barrel on the windrose
- **Warehouses** allow extra retention as per building text
- If you lack space, you **choose** which goods to store and return the rest to supply

Goods acquired in **other** phases sit on the windrose without a storage cap **until** the next captain phase; **storage is enforced only at the end of the captain phase**.

#### End of phase

Captain’s duty: unload **full** ships to supply. **Partially full** (and empty) ships stay until the next captain — future captain phases are tighter (kinds and space already committed).

#### Notes

- Multiple turns per player are normal

##### Worked example — captain phase (4 players, from the rulebook)

**Setup:** Anna is captain. She has **2 corn** and **6 sugar**. The **5-** and **7-**space ships are empty; the **6-**space ship already holds **3 corn**.

- Anna must load **either** corn or sugar (one kind per turn). She chooses **sugar** and must load as many as possible: all **6** on the **7**-ship (she may **not** use the 5-ship and leave 1 sugar behind). She earns **6 VP** + **1** captain privilege = **7 VP**.

- **Bob** is next. He has **2 sugar** and **3 tobacco**. He loads **1 sugar** onto the **7**-ship, **filling** it (the second sugar has no ship). He could instead load **3 tobacco** onto the empty **5**-ship; the rulebook notes he might keep tobacco for the trader — he earns **1 VP** for the sugar he loaded.

- **Chris** has **2 corn** and **1 tobacco**. She loads **1 tobacco** onto the **5**-ship → **1 VP**.

- **David** has **1 corn** and **5 indigo**. He has **no legal way to load indigo** in this situation (per the rulebook diagram). He **must** load his **1 corn** onto the **6**-ship (with the 3 corn already there) → **1 VP**.

- **Anna** again: she must load her **2 corn** on the **6**-ship → **2 VP**.

- **Bob** must load **3 tobacco** onto the **5**-ship → **3 VP**.

- Chris and David still hold goods but **no legal load** remains for anyone. Loading ends.

**After loading:** Chris and David **store** leftovers: each may keep **1** barrel on the windrose unless warehouses apply; if they still have too many goods, they **choose** which to keep and return the rest to supply. Anna **unloads every full ship** to the supply. **Partially full** ships stay: e.g. **4 tobacco** can remain on the **5**-space ship into the next captain phase.

---

### Prospector

- **Action:** None
- **Privilege:** Gain **1 doubloon** from the bank (plus any doubloons on the role card)

## Game end

The game ends at the **end of the round** in which **any** of these becomes true (check **when** stated):


| Trigger            | When checked                                                                                       |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Colonists**      | **End of mayor phase:** not enough colonists in supply to refill the colonist ship **as required** |
| **12 city spaces** | **During builder phase:** a player **builds** on their **12th** city space                         |
| **VP chips**       | **During captain phase:** the **last** VP chip is taken from supply                                |


If VP chips run out mid-game, track further VP with **pencil and paper**; final scoring includes those recorded VPs.

---

## Final scoring

Each player totals:

- VP chips (including any recorded on paper after chips ran out)
- **Printed VP** on every building (red-brown number) — **always**, whether occupied or not
- **Bonus VP** from the five unique large buildings’ **special scoring rules** (Guild Hall, Residence, Fortress, Customs House, City Hall) — **only when that large building is occupied**

The printed VP on the five large buildings counts like any other building; the **extra** scoring described on those tiles applies only when **occupied**.

### Tie breaker

Most combined **doubloons + goods** (1 good = 1 doubloon).

## Buildings

### General rules

- Each building may be built **once** per player
- **Occupied** = at least one colonist in the building. **Only occupied buildings “function”** for abilities — **exception:** printed **victory points** count whether occupied or not
- Where a building sits in the city has no effect on rules
- A large building needs **two adjacent empty** spaces; it counts as **one** building
- You may **move** a building within your city to make room for a large building; you may **not** remove a building from the city to free space or prolong the game
- **Cost** is the number in the first circle; **VP** is the red-brown number (upper area). Cost is irrelevant after building

### Base game — printed cost & VP (reference)

**Cost** = doubloons to the bank when built (before builder privilege and quarry discounts). **VP** = printed victory points on the tile. Values match standard **base-game** tiles; always confirm against your physical components.

Source: [BoardGameHelpers — Puerto Rico Building Reference](https://www.boardgamehelpers.com/PuertoRico/BuildingReference.aspx) (base-game entries only; expansion buildings omitted).

**Optional house rule:** Some groups swap **University** and **Factory** printed costs (**7** ↔ **8**), per designer-suggested variant (see links on that reference page).

### Production buildings

There are no separate “raw material” pieces — production yields **finished goods barrels** only. Unused colonists on plantations or on empty production circles **produce nothing**.


| Building        | Produces |
| --------------- | -------- |
| Indigo Plant    | Indigo   |
| Sugar Mill      | Sugar    |
| Tobacco Storage | Tobacco  |
| Coffee Roaster  | Coffee   |


**Corn:** No production building. Occupied **corn** plantations produce **corn** directly in the craftsman phase.

**Production limits:**

- Circles on the building = **max** barrels **if** those circles have colonists
- You also need enough **occupied** matching **plantations** (each plantation supplies at most one barrel per type per craftsman)

**Example:** 3 corn plantations but only 2 occupied → 2 corn. Tobacco storage with 2 circles colonized but only 1 tobacco plantation occupied → 1 tobacco. Fourth sugar plantation empty → caps sugar at 3 even if the mill has more circles.

### Violet buildings

17 total: 12 small (2 copies each), 5 large (1 each). Special functions **break normal rules**; using them is **optional** (important for **Wharf**).

---

### Small Market

When the owner **sells** in the trader phase: **+1** doubloon from the bank.

**Example:** Anna sells **corn** (trader price **0**) and still receives **+1** doubloon from the Small Market.

---

### Hacienda

On your settler turn, **before** taking a face-up plantation, you may take the **top** face-down plantation and place it on an **empty** island space. Then finish the rest of your settler turn: **other players** take one face-up plantation (or a quarry with **Construction Hut**); the **role chooser** takes one face-up plantation **or** uses the settler privilege for a quarry (if legal).

- If you take a face-down tile, you **must place it** — **no discarding**
- **Hacienda + Construction Hut:** You may **not** take a quarry **instead** of that face-down tile. With both, you can still take **at most one quarry** in the phase (hacienda draw is not swapped for quarry)

---

### Construction Hut

In the settler phase, may place a **quarry** instead of taking a face-up **plantation** (subject to quarry availability).

- **Note:** If you also have **Hacienda**, see Hacienda — **one quarry** total for the phase when both interact

---

### Small Warehouse

After the captain phase, besides **1** barrel on the windrose, you may keep **all barrels of one chosen kind** (they stay on the windrose; the tile is just the rule reminder).

Warehouses do **not** exempt you from **having to load** onto ships when the captain requires it.

---

### Hospice

When you place a **plantation or quarry** in the settler phase, you may take **1 colonist** from **supply** and place it **on that new tile**.

- **Hospice + Hacienda:** You **do not** get a colonist for the **extra** face-down hacienda tile — only for the tile you place as your normal settler pick (or privilege quarry)
- If supply is empty: take from **colonist ship**; if both empty, skip

---

### Office

When selling in the trader phase, your good need **not** differ from goods already in the trading house.

- If the trading house is **full (4)**, you **cannot** sell there
- **Two offices:** Each occupied office owner may sell a duplicate kind on their turn (example: house has tobacco; Bob sells tobacco with office; Chris with the other office can also sell tobacco)

---

### Large Market

When selling: **+2** doubloons from the bank.

- **Small + Large Market both occupied:** **+3** total when you sell

---

### Small Warehouse + Large Warehouse

- **Large Warehouse:** After captain, besides 1 on windrose, keep **all** barrels of **two** chosen kinds
- **Both warehouses:** Keep **all** barrels of **three** chosen kinds (still plus the default 1-barrel windrose rule as per rulebook structure)

---

### Factory

If you produce goods of **more than one kind** in the craftsman phase: earn from bank by **number of kinds** (barrel counts per kind do not matter):


| Kinds produced | Doubloons |
| -------------- | --------- |
| 2              | 1         |
| 3              | 2         |
| 4              | 3         |
| 5              | 5         |


**Example (supply limits production):** Many plantations/buildings set up, but supply only allows **2 sugar** and **1 tobacco** to actually be produced → **2 kinds** → **1** doubloon.

**Example (David, from the rulebook):** David has an occupied **Factory**, **3** occupied corn plantations, **3** occupied sugar plantations, **1** occupied tobacco plantation, and the matching production buildings with enough colonists. Because the **corn** supply is empty and only **2 sugar** barrels remain in supply, he actually produces **2 sugar** and **1 tobacco** only → **2 kinds** → **1** doubloon from the Factory (barrel counts per kind do not change the payout table).

---

### University

In the **builder** phase, when you **build** a building: take **1 colonist** from **supply** and place it **on the University**.

- If the new building has **multiple** circles, you still get only **one** colonist
- Supply empty → colonist from **ship**; both empty → skip

---

### Harbor

Each time in the **captain** phase you **load** goods onto a **real cargo ship**, gain **+1 VP** (each load instance that loads onto a ship — track with chips/paper).

**Example (Harbor + Wharf, from the rulebook):** With both buildings occupied, you load **3** of **5 tobacco** onto the real tobacco ship (the ship fills with those **3**) → **3 VP** for barrels **+ 1 VP** Harbor. Later you load **2 sugar** onto the sugar ship → **2 + 1** VP. Later you use **Wharf** to send the remaining **2 tobacco** to supply, scoring as if shipped → **2 + 1** VP (Harbor counts each such “load,” including the Wharf use). **Total:** **3 VP** from Harbor bonuses this phase, and **2 VP** from the tobacco you cleared via Wharf (as ordinary shipping VP for those barrels).

---

### Wharf

Once per **captain phase**, when you **must** load, you may instead send **all** barrels of **one chosen kind** from your windrose **to the supply** and score VP **as if** they shipped on a ship (unlimited capacity “imaginary ship”).

- **When to use it:** You may choose **which** captain-phase turn (if any) to use your single Wharf use. On that turn, using the Wharf satisfies the “must load” obligation instead of putting those barrels on a **cargo** ship.
- Wharf barrels go **back to supply**, so they do **not** use space on the three cargo ships (those ships can still be empty or hold other kinds as usual)
- **Must use wharf completely:** You must place **all** barrels you hold of the kind you choose (you need not choose the kind you have the most of)
- If you use the wharf, you are **not** loading those barrels onto a cargo ship — real ships may stay unfilled and not empty at phase end. **Example:** If you Wharf **all 5** tobacco at once, the real “tobacco” ship might still be **partially full** at phase end and is **not** emptied.
- **Imaginary ship vs other players:** Your imaginary ship may carry a good that is already on one of the three cargo ships **or** the same kind another player shipped with **their** Wharf this phase (those are separate imaginary ships).
- Rulebook notes an **11-barrel** capacity for the imaginary wharf ship (reference limit from original rules)

---

### Large buildings (five unique)

Each needs **two adjacent** spaces; each counts as **one** building. In these rules, “large building” means these five.

#### Guild Hall

If **occupied** at game end: **+1 VP** per **small** production building in your city (occupied or not), **+2 VP** per **large** production building (occupied or not).  
*Small = small indigo / small sugar; Large = indigo plant, sugar mill, tobacco storage, coffee roaster.*

**Example:** At game end you have Guild Hall occupied plus a **small** sugar mill, a **large** sugar mill, a **small** indigo plant, and a **coffee roaster**. Small production buildings → **+1 +1**; large production buildings → **+2 +2** = **+6 VP** from Guild Hall (plus the tile’s printed VP).

#### Residence

If **occupied** at game end: bonus VP from **filled island spaces** (plantations + quarries):  
9 spaces → 4 VP · 10 → 5 VP · 11 → 6 VP · **12** → **7 VP**

**Example:** **10** island spaces filled with plantations and/or quarries → **+5 VP** (plus printed VP).

#### Fortress

If **occupied** at game end: **+1 VP** per **3 colonists** on your board (plantations, quarries, buildings, San Juan — total).

**Example:** **20** colonists on your board (plantations, quarries, buildings, San Juan) → **+6 VP** (one VP per **full 3** colonists: **6 × 3 = 18**, remainder **2** ignored).

#### Customs House

If **occupied** at game end: **+1 VP** per **4 VP** you earned **from VP chips** during the game (include VP recorded on **paper** after chips ran out **before** game end). **Do not** count building printed VP or large-building bonuses in this divisor.

**Example:** **23 VP** from chips (and eligible paper) during the game → **+5 VP** (23 ÷ 4 = 5 remainder 3; only **full sets of 4** count).

#### City Hall

If **occupied** at game end: **+1 VP** per **violet** building in your city (occupied or not). **City Hall itself** counts.

**Example:** City Hall plus **hacienda, harbor, office, construction hut, large warehouse, residence** → **7** violet buildings → **+7 VP** (plus printed VP).
